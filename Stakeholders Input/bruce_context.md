# Weekly digest brief: Bruce Haryali, AtliQ Hardware

## Who I am and how I read this
- I am Bruce Haryali. I run AtliQ Hardware. We sell PCs, peripherals, accessories, networking and storage
  in 15 markets across 5 regions (APAC, EU, North America, LATAM, MEA), through three channels:
  E-Commerce, Retailer and Distributor.
- I read this digest on Monday morning, on my phone, in under 2 minutes.
- I use it to make decisions. Every digest must answer four questions:
  1. Are we on track?
  2. If not, what is it costing us in money?
  3. What is the real cause?
  4. What do I need to decide, and who acts by when?
- If everything is fine, say so in one line. Do not invent problems.

## The numbers I watch (the week that just ended, Monday to Sunday)

| Metric | Compare with | Flag it when |
|---|---|---|
| Net sales | The week before, and the weekly target | More than 10% below the week before, or more than 10% below target |
| Net sales spike | The week before | More than 15% above the week before (good news, still explain it) |
| Gross margin % | The week before | Down more than 2 percentage points |
| Units sold | The week before | Only mention it if it moves more than 15% |

Definitions (the numbers are given to you; never recalculate them):
- Net sales = revenue after all discounts and deductions.
- Gross margin % = gross margin / net sales x 100.
- Gross margin $ = the money left after costs. The table shows it, and the money impact line
  ("If this continues") is calculated for you.
- Target = the sum of the daily targets for that week.

## Money first
- Always say what a flagged change costs or earns in dollars, using the gross margin $ and
  net sales numbers you are given. I decide on money, not on percentage points.
- If the change continues, say what it means per month (the monthly number is given to you).

## How to find the real cause
Only investigate numbers that break a rule above. Go from broad to narrow and stop as soon
as you reach a cause someone can act on:

1. Region: which region moved the most?
2. Market: inside that region, which market?
3. Channel: E-Commerce, Retailer or Distributor?
4. Customer: is one customer causing most of it?
5. Product: which segment or category?

If the change is about the same in every region, it is company-wide. Then compare with the
week before, product by product (like for like), so a change in product mix does not hide
or shrink the real move:
- Cost per unit = manufacturing_cost / sold_qty, per category
- Price per unit = gross_sales / sold_qty, per category
- Discount % = post_invoice_discount / net_invoice_sales x 100
- Pre-invoice deduction % = pre_invoice_deduction / gross_sales x 100

Also find out:
- Scope: is it all regions and all categories, or only some? Name them.
- Start: did it start on one specific day (a step change) or build up slowly? Check day by day.
- Still going: is the last day of the week still affected?

Always name the kind of cause: volume (we sold less), price, discounts, or cost.

## Only real causes
- Report a cause only if it explains at least 20% of the change. Say roughly how much of the
  change it explains, for example "explains about 90% of the margin drop".
- Small moves (for example a discount going from 10.0% to 10.6%) are noise. Leave them out.
  A false cause leads to a wrong decision, which is worse than no cause.
- If you cannot find a cause in the data, say so plainly. Never guess.

## Known patterns: do not treat these as problems
- The last 3 days of every month are about 15% higher. A week with month-end is usually about
  6% higher, and the week after is lower. That is normal.
- Weekends are lower than weekdays.
- If the week before was unusual (for example a seasonal spike), a drop this week can be a
  return to normal. Check this first, and say it clearly instead of calling it a problem.
- Normal weeks run about 3% below target. Only flag target gaps bigger than 10%.
- Sudden daily drops in one market and channel are escalated to the local owner every morning.
  Mention them here in one line only if they moved the weekly total.

## What a good reason looks like
- Good: "MEA margin fell from 35.0% to 30.0% because freight cost per unit rose 20%,
  which explains about 85% of the drop."
- Good: "Brazil Retailer sales fell 40% because one customer placed no orders all week."
- Bad: "Sales were impacted by various market dynamics." (no number, no cause)
- Bad: "Margin may have dropped due to costs." (a guess, not checked in the data)
- Bad: "Discounts rose slightly, adding pressure." (too small to matter, leads to a wrong action)

## Key insights: new facts only
- Do not repeat numbers that are already in the table.
- Use insights for what the table cannot show: scope (where), start (since when),
  whether it is still going, and the biggest affected area.

## Actions I want
- At most 2, and only for causes you confirmed in the data.
- Each action must have:
  - Owner: a role, for example Region Sales Head, Country Manager, Channel Lead, COO or CFO.
  - Deadline: a specific day this week, for example "by Friday".
  - Expected effect: what it recovers or protects, in dollars per week if possible.
  - Trade-off: the cost, time or risk, in a few words.
- If nothing broke a rule, give no actions, or one small one.

## Decision needed
- End with one line: the decision I need to make, as a clear choice.
  Example: "Approve a supplier price review, or accept about $1.0M less margin per week."
- If nothing needs a decision, say "No decision needed this week."

## How to write it
- Headline: one sentence, max 20 words, the single most important point, with the money impact.
- Key insights: max 3, max 20 words each, new facts only.
- Why: max 3, max 25 words each, every one quoting a number and how much of the change it explains.
- Money in millions with one decimal ($29.9M). Percentages with one decimal (16.9%).
  Margin changes in points (down 2.4 points).
- Plain simple English. No jargon. No em dashes. No filler like "it is important to note".

## Style example (made-up numbers, never copy them)
Headline: Margin fell 3.0 points, costing about $0.9M this week, as LATAM freight costs jumped.
Insights:
- Only LATAM is affected; every other region held its margin.
- Started on Tuesday and was still there on Sunday.
Why:
- LATAM freight cost per unit rose from $4.0 to $6.5, explaining about 90% of the margin drop.
Actions:
- Renegotiate LATAM freight rates. Owner: LATAM Sales Head. By: Thursday.
  Effect: recovers about $0.8M per week. Trade-off: may need a longer contract.
Decision needed: Approve the freight renegotiation, or raise LATAM prices by 2%.
